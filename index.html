How the Signal Score works

Five steps, run every week.

01

Collect

New rounds come in weekly from public reporting. Nothing paywalled or login-gated, so every deal traces back to something you can read yourself.

02

Standardize

Each round is reduced to the same set of fields — amount, stage, sector, region, investors. Facts, never article prose. Nothing is comparable until everything has the same shape.

03

Match to a cohort

The round is grouped with the deals it should actually be judged against: same stage, same sector, same window of time. This is the cohort, and it is what makes the rest of it mean anything.

04

Score

The Signal Score places the round inside that cohort — large or small, hot or cooling, relative to its genuine peers rather than to the market at large.

05

Review

Nothing publishes without a human sign-off. The approval gate is the control, not a formality.

Thin cohort, no Signal Score. A number you can't defend is worse than no number.

How to read a Signal Score

What the number means, and what it doesn't.

Relative, not absolute A high score means large for its cohort. It says nothing about whether the company is good.
Coverage, not the market Built from rounds that got reported. Large here means large relative to what gets written about.
A filter, not a verdict It tells you where to look first. You still do the work.
